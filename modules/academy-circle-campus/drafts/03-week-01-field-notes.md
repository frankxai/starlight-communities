Most broken AI projects do not fail because the model was too weak. They fail because nobody separated **reversible preparation** from **irreversible execution** before wiring tools together.

Your first exercise in Starlight takes **20 minutes total**—15 minutes of unaided thinking before asking any AI assistant, and 5 minutes to post your structured note below.

## The triage rule

Take one recurring workflow from your work or creative practice (keep all details synthetic or public-safe) and classify it into one of three lanes:

- **`BOUNDED_AUTOMATE`** — Internal, reversible preparation with a hard step limit (for example: clustering synthetic bug reports or checking markdown link syntax).
- **`ASSIST_ONLY`** — Drafts or recommendations staged for a named human reviewer before any external send, publish action, or customer touchpoint.
- **`DO_NOT_AUTOMATE`** — Any path where autonomous execution would touch spend, pricing, legal commitments, credentials, or clinical/financial judgment without human authority.

## Worked synthetic example

Here is what a crisp, high-signal Week 1 Field Note looks like:

```text
1. Lens: Structured Workflow Operator
2. Candidate workflow: Inbound partner renewal email replies (synthetic B2B SaaS ops)
3. Verdict: ASSIST_ONLY (never autonomous send)
4. Human gate & boundary: Agent drafts reply context from public docs into a review queue; Account Owner approves every outbound email; zero pricing or discount authority.
5. Kill criterion & rollback: If fewer than 35% of staged drafts are accepted with minor edits across 30 reviewed tickets, disable drafting and revert to static templates.
```

Notice what makes that useful: a partner can read it in thirty seconds, test whether a prompt injection could trigger an outbound discount, and verify the exact number (`< 35%` across `30` tickets) that turns the workflow off.
